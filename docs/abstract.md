# Abstract

**Watching a Liquid Freeze: GPU-Accelerated Parallel Molecular Dynamics with In Situ Machine-Learning Phase Detection**

Manas Vardhan, CSCI 596 Final Project, Fall 2026

**(1a) Problem.** Crystallization of a supercooled liquid begins with a rare, short-lived event: a
nanometer-sized crystal nucleus forms somewhere in a large volume of disordered atoms and either
dissolves or grows. Molecular dynamics (MD) can resolve this atom by atom, but the event's place and
time are unknown in advance, so the usual workflow writes the full trajectory to disk at high
frequency and searches it afterwards. **(1b)** At scale, this makes I/O, not computation, the
bottleneck, and most of the stored data describes nothing interesting.

**(2) Approach and finding.** We build a GPU-accelerated spatial-decomposition MD code (MPI + CUDA)
for a Lennard-Jones system and split its processes into simulation ranks and dedicated in situ
analysis ranks. While the simulation runs, the analysis ranks compute Steinhardt bond-order features
for every atom and pass them through a small neural network, trained on data generated by the same
code, to label each atom as liquid or crystal. They track the largest crystalline nucleus over time
and write only these labels and summaries. We measure the GPU speedup, the parallel scaling, the cost of
in situ analysis as a fraction of the simulation step, and the disk output it avoids. We also show the
nucleus growing in an OVITO visualization.

**(3) So what?** The pattern (simulate on GPUs and understand the result in flight, while storing only
what matters) carries over to any large simulation whose interesting events are rare and whose raw
output is too large to keep.
